1. The reason to build the file · 0:00–0:35 estimated

Visual cue: Presenter to camera. Show the genuine Cheq Yourself wordmark, then the three record names. Branding artwork may illustrate the idea; do not present it as a real client file.

Your home may be one of the most important things you pass on to your family. Alongside it, you can pass on an organized record of its history, rather than leave someone else to start the research from scratch.

I'm Mark Smith from Cheq Yourself. In this short overview, I want to show you where that research starts, what our documentation service includes, and how to decide whether it's a fit for your property.

2. Start with three records · 0:35–1:20 estimated

Visual cue: Show three simple labels: Certificate of Title → Survey → Crown Grant. Use illustrative labels, not invented legal documents or client identifiers.

The starting point is three records: your Certificate of Title, your survey and the original Crown Grant.

The title is where you begin looking at the current record and the references to earlier titles. The survey helps you understand the recorded parcel and its boundary information. The Crown Grant is the original grant, with its own terms, reservations and exceptions.

Those records need to be read together. The work is in finding what's available, following the references and preparing the documentation around the particular property.

3. Choose whether to do it yourself · 1:20–1:50 estimated

Visual cue: Two neutral labels: Do the research yourself / Commission the preparation. No fear imagery, before-and-after legal claim or scarcity counter.

You can do that research yourself. That's a valid choice, and the webinar explains the starting points.

But if you want the work prepared for you, that's why we offer the Assert Crown Grant Documentation Package. You're commissioning property-specific research and documentation across six defined work areas.

4. Explain the six work areas · 1:50–2:50 estimated

Visual cue: Reveal the six approved work areas one at a time. Hold the fourth label long enough to read 'where records allow'.

First, Chain of Title Discovery: tracing the predecessor references through the available records.

Second, Crown Grant investigation: locating and examining the original grant.

Third, current title and survey review.

Fourth, creation of a working metes-and-bounds description where records allow. That's a working boundary description from the records, not a new survey.

Fifth, Acceptance Documentation.

And sixth, constructive notice documentation to government and community.

The written scope confirms the work for your property. You still provide the necessary information, review the documents and complete the signing, mailing or publication steps identified in that scope.
